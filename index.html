FIELD NOTE 01 PHASE 01: SCIENCE BRIEFING

First, know what you're looking at.

A stain is a clue, not a complete identity. Let's review the structures that help microbiologists interpret a cell.

CELL

Cell membrane

A selectively permeable boundary that regulates movement of substances into and out of the cell. Both bacteria and archaea have cell membranes, though their membrane chemistry differs.

WALL

Cell wall

Provides structural support and helps resist osmotic pressure in many microbes. Bacterial walls commonly contain peptidoglycan; not every microbe has a cell wall.

GRAM

Gram staining

Separates many bacteria into Gram-positive and Gram-negative groups based on cell-envelope properties and stain retention. It does not identify a species by itself.

i

SCIENTIFIC CAUTION
A Gram-stain result can be affected by culture age, sample quality, and staining technique. Never infer more than the method supports.
